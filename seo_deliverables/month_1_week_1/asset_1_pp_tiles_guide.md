## 2. What Are PP Interlocking Sports Tiles?

**Snapshot:** PP interlocking sports tiles are high-impact, UV-stabilised polypropylene copolymer modular tiles measuring 12x12 inches. They click together without adhesives to form a seamless, self-draining, flexible sports surface that handles heavy impact and extreme weather conditions.

Unlike rolled flooring or poured-in-place rubber, PP modular tiles are rigid but flexible squares that lock together using an engineered peg-and-loop system. Because they require no glue or permanent chemical bonding to the sub-base, they "float" above the concrete. This floating nature allows the grid to expand and contract naturally with temperature changes. The open-grid surface design ensures that rainwater passes directly through the tile and flows away across the sub-base slope, eliminating surface puddles and making the court playable within minutes after heavy rain.

## 4. Sub-Base Requirements and Preparation

**Snapshot:** PP interlocking tiles require a flat, hard sub-base, typically a minimum M20 grade concrete slab or compacted asphalt. The sub-base must include a 1% slope to ensure water drains effectively beneath the floating tile grid.

A sports court is only as good as its foundation. PP tiles are not designed to be laid over grass, dirt, sand, or uneven gravel. The modular tiles conform to the surface they rest on; therefore, if the concrete is wavy or has significant dips, the finished court will mirror those imperfections. 

For a successful installation:
- **Material:** Concrete (M20 grade minimum) or well-compacted asphalt.
- **Finish:** Flat and smooth (trowel finish for concrete).
- **Drainage:** A mandatory 1% slope in one or two directions. Because the tiles are porous, rain falls through them. The sub-base must guide that water off the court footprint.
- **Curing:** Concrete must be fully cured (typically 28 days) before installing tiles to prevent moisture trapping, though tiles are more forgiving of moisture than acrylic coatings.

## 6. PP Tiles vs. Acrylic Coating: Which Is Better?

**Snapshot:** PP tiles are superior for high-impact multi-sport school courts and areas prone to concrete cracking, as the floating tiles hide sub-base flaws. Acrylic coatings are better for dedicated professional tennis courts requiring specific ITF pace classifications.

Institutional buyers frequently debate between these two outdoor solutions. 

**Choose PP Interlocking Tiles when:**
- The court will be used for basketball, skating, and casual multi-sport activities.
- The area experiences heavy rainfall (tiles drain instantly).
- The existing concrete has minor structural cracks (the floating tiles bridge small gaps without translating the crack to the surface).
- You need a zero-maintenance solution.

**Choose Acrylic Court Systems when:**
- Building a dedicated tennis court where ITF (International Tennis Federation) ball pace is the primary concern.
- You have a perfectly flawless, newly laid concrete or asphalt base.
- You want a seamless, painted aesthetic.

## 7. Overcoming The Summer Heat Objection: Will They Warp?

**Snapshot:** Low-grade generic tiles warp in Indian summers because they lack thermal expansion joints. U Win PP tiles use an engineered expansion-gap interlocking system that safely accommodates thermal expansion up to 60°C without buckling.

A common fear among facility managers is that plastic tiles will buckle and form dangerous trip hazards under the fierce Indian sun. This happens exclusively with cheap, inflexible tiles sold by traders. 

Physics dictates that plastic expands when heated. U Win Floortech tiles are designed with built-in micro-gaps within the locking mechanism. When the tiles heat up in May and June, these gaps allow each individual tile to expand slightly without pushing against its neighbour. The entire court breathes. Combined with heavy-duty UV stabilisers that prevent the material from drying out and becoming brittle, the surface remains perfectly flat and safe year-round.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What sub-base is required for PP interlocking sports tiles?**
A: PP interlocking modular tiles require a level, flat, hard sub-base — either a concrete slab (minimum M20 grade, minimum 100mm thickness) or a well-compacted asphalt base. The sub-base must slope at 1% to allow self-drainage through the tile grid. Soft, uneven, or grass bases are not suitable.

**Q: Can PP modular tiles be used for indoor sports halls?**
A: Yes. While they are famous for outdoor resilience, flat-top PP tiles are excellent for indoor multi-sport halls, gymnasiums, and skating rinks. However, for dedicated professional indoor badminton, U Win recommends our PVC vinyl or Canadian Maple wooden flooring.

**Q: How do you clean and maintain a PP tile court?**
A: Maintenance is extremely low. Routine leaf blowing or sweeping keeps the surface clear. For dust and dirt buildup, the court can simply be pressure-washed or hosed down. The water will drain straight through the grid and off the court slope. No waxing, recoating, or chemical treatments are required.
