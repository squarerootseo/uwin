## 8. DOS AND DONTS — NON-NEGOTIABLE EXECUTION RULES

### ALWAYS DO THIS
- Always use port 4322 (`http://localhost:4322`) when running the local development server for preview purposes.
- Naturally weave in 3-4 internal links to relevant service pages or other blog posts within the main body of every blog asset.
- Apply the Snapshot Rule to every H2 and H3. After every heading, write a 40-60 word direct, citation-ready answer before any elaboration.
- Cite specific specifications. Say 13mm IAAF-specification EPDM sandwich track and Canadian Maple kiln-dried sports-grade wooden flooring.
- Name the target institution type in every piece. Schools, universities, developers, clubs, badminton academies.
- Include a WhatsApp CTA in every content asset. Minimum 3 times per long-form piece. Phone: +91 98216 48112.
- Explain sub-base requirements whenever describing any product.
- Reference certifications by the full standard name the first time.
- Distinguish wood species correctly. Always name whether it is Canadian Maple or Teakwood and state the specific performance difference.
- Distinguish PVC vinyl from wooden flooring clearly. PVC vinyl = performance surface, more affordable, indoor. Wooden = premium, tournament-standard, longer lifespan.

### NEVER DO THIS
- Never use the brand name (e.g. UWIN Floortech) in the meta title for blog pages.
- Never use banned filler phrases: high-quality, world-class, best sports flooring company, cutting-edge, seamless solutions, innovative, game-changer.
- Never publish a content asset under 1,500 words. The topical authority standard is 2,500 words.
- Never use stock photography of sports courts.
- Never confuse EPDM with SBR in content. SBR is the base layer; EPDM is the top wear layer.
- Never suggest acrylic coating can replace a structurally damaged base.
- Never use artificial turf and synthetic grass interchangeably without context — clarify sand-based vs. rubber-crumb types.
- Never confuse badminton PVC vinyl with general PVC flooring.
- Never create content without a CTA.

## 18. FAQ INTELLIGENCE BANK (SCHEMA-READY)

All answers structured for FAQPage JSON-LD schema.

Q: What sub-base is required for PP interlocking sports tiles?
A: PP interlocking modular tiles require a level, flat, hard sub-base — either a concrete slab (minimum M20 grade, minimum 100mm thickness) or a well-compacted asphalt base. The sub-base must slope at 1% to allow self-drainage through the tile grid. Soft, uneven, or grass bases are not suitable.

Q: What is the difference between SBR rubber and EPDM rubber flooring?
A: SBR (Styrene-Butadiene Rubber) is made from recycled vehicle tyres and used as a cost-effective cushion base layer in sandwich track systems. EPDM (Ethylene Propylene Diene Monomer) is a virgin synthetic rubber compound that is UV-resistant, colour-stable, and durable for outdoor applications. EPDM is used for the top wear layer in high-quality running tracks and play areas.

Q: How many layers does a proper acrylic sports court have?
A: A properly built acrylic sports court includes a minimum of 5 layers: concrete primer, acrylic resurfacer, two colour wear coats with silica sand texture, and court line marking paint. Premium systems include 3 additional cushion coat layers for shock absorption, bringing the total to 8 layers. Skipping any layer — particularly the primer — leads to surface delamination within 12-18 months.

Q: What is the difference between PVC vinyl and wooden badminton court flooring?
A: PVC vinyl badminton flooring is a multi-layered synthetic surface with a foam backing for shock absorption — the correct choice for multi-sport halls, humid climates, and budget-conscious institutions. Wooden badminton flooring (Canadian Maple or Teakwood) is the international tournament standard delivering superior ball bounce, a lifespan of 15-25 years, and the professional feel demanded by BWF-affiliated clubs. PVC vinyl suits training facilities; wooden flooring suits tournament courts.

Q: What is the difference between Canadian Maple and Teakwood for badminton courts?
A: Canadian Maple is the international BWF standard — used in World Championships and Olympic facilities. It has a uniform, dense grain structure delivering consistent ball bounce and high tensile strength. Teakwood is a premium Indian alternative with high natural oil content, providing natural moisture resistance and a rich aesthetic. Canadian Maple is preferred for high-performance training and tournament play; Teakwood suits premium institutional facilities where aesthetics and durability are priorities.

Q: What is sand-based artificial grass and where is it used?
A: Sand-based artificial grass uses silica sand as the infill between synthetic fibres, keeping them upright and providing controlled foot traction. Unlike rubber-crumb infill, silica sand looks natural, feels soft underfoot, and produces no heat build-up in Indian summers. It is used for outdoor tennis courts, padel courts, school sports fields, public parks, and residential society recreational lawns. Courts are playable almost immediately after rain.

Q: How long does PVC vinyl badminton flooring last?
A: A properly installed, well-maintained PVC vinyl badminton sports floor lasts 10-15 years under normal institutional use. Lifespan depends on daily usage hours, cleaning practices, and protection from heavy equipment. Wooden badminton flooring lasts 15-25 years with refinishing every 3-5 years.

Q: Can U Win supply and install a badminton court for a residential society clubhouse?
A: Yes. UWIN Floortech supplies and installs PVC vinyl sports flooring for indoor badminton courts in residential society clubhouses. We assess the existing concrete sub-floor, supply the correct grade of PVC vinyl, and complete installation including court line marking. WhatsApp us at +91 98216 48112 with your room dimensions and we respond within 4 working hours with a specification and cost estimate.

Q: Can U Win acrylic flooring be applied over an existing concrete court?
A: Yes. Existing concrete courts can be resurfaced using the U Win acrylic coating system, provided the base is structurally sound, clean, and level. Cosmetic cracks are filled using an acrylic crack filler before the primer coat. Structural cracks or significant base subsidence require base repair before coating can proceed.

Q: What maintenance does an EPDM running track require?
A: EPDM running tracks require minimal maintenance. Routine sweeping (monthly), pressure washing (quarterly), and inspection for joint separation or granule loss (annual) are standard protocols. Avoid petroleum-based solvents. Spot repair with matching EPDM mixture should be done promptly to prevent water ingress.
